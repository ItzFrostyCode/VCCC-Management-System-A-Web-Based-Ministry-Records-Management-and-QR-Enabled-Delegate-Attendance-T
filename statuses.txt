
Potter’s House Fellowship and Pastoral Roles
The Potter’s House Christian Fellowship (PHCF) is a global Pentecostal network founded in 1970 by Wayman O. Mitchell in Prescott, AZ
. It now comprises thousands of autonomous “Potter’s House” churches (often called The Potter’s House, The Door, Victory Chapel, Christian Center, etc.) worldwide
. Each local church has a senior pastor and various associate pastors (e.g. Spanish Pastor, Youth Pastor, etc.) who lead the congregation. For example, the Prescott Potter’s House site lists roles like “Senior Pastor”, “Associate Pastor”, “Spanish Pastor”, “Youth Pastor”, etc. with bios of each leader
. Pastors in this fellowship are often sent or appointed from other churches – some “pioneered” new congregations, others “assumed” (took over) existing pastorates, and some “returned” from mission assignments.

Pastoral Lifecycle States (Statuses)
Pastors in PHCF churches move through a well‑defined ministry lifecycle. The following statuses are commonly used to describe a pastor’s situation or how they assumed their position:

PIONEERING. The pastor is the founding pastor of a new church plant. For example, one Potter’s House biography describes pastors being “launched out… to establish a new church in Albany, New York,” i.e. they pioneered that congregation
. Another profile notes “They started with pioneering a church in Falmouth, Massachusetts” before later taking on other pastorates
.

TAKEOVER. The pastor takes over an existing pastorate from a previous pastor. Churches often say a new pastor “assumed the pastorate” of a congregation. For instance, Keith Sullivan’s bio says he “assumed the pastorate of Victory Chapel in Chicopee, Massachusetts” after his pioneering work
. Similarly, a UK Potters House site notes, “In April of 2016 they took over the pastorate of the Reading congregation”
.

TRANSFERRED. The pastor moves from one assignment to another. This might mean they left one church to serve at another church within the network. It’s similar to a change of station. For example, the Prescott church’s senior pastor Greg Mitchell “returned to Prescott” after mission service abroad, indicating he was transferred back home
. (We treat TRANSFERRED as an end-of-assignment reason on the source church and a beginning on the new one.)

PULLOUT. The pastor withdraws from an assignment (often from a missionary or planted church) before its normal completion. This could be due to personal/family reasons or a strategic decision. Pulled out pastors are no longer serving that church, but the church continues (usually under a new pastor).

ACTIVE. The pastor is currently serving in their assignment (i.e. they have no end date). This is the default “normal” status while in a pastorate.

DECEASED. The pastor has died. (For example, founding Pastor Wayman Mitchell “passed away Sept. 21, 2020”
.) A deceased pastor is no longer active and typically has no current assignment.

UNDEPLOYED. The pastor is not currently assigned to any church (perhaps awaiting assignment or retired). They are still “on staff” in the database, but not leading a congregation.

REDIRECTION. The pastor was redirected by leadership from one assignment to another, often mid-term. In practice, this often overlaps with TRANSFERRED, but may imply a direct directive rather than a voluntary move.

These statuses help categorize each pastor’s assignment history. In practice, we record them as either an attribute on the pastor’s record or on each specific assignment record. For example, a pastor’s current status might be “ACTIVE” or “UNDEPLOYED”, while each historical assignment can have a type like PIONEERING or TAKEOVER and an end reason (TRANSFERRED, PULLOUT, etc.). Potter’s House profiles frequently describe these roles narratively – e.g. “They pioneered a church in Aurora… then pastored the Potter’s House in Twentynine Palms, CA”
 – so our system should capture those transitions in structured form.

Proposed Data Model and Schema Changes
Given the old schema, we should normalize pastors vs. assignments. Recommended tables/fields:

Pastors (Person table). Stores personal info for each pastor: ID, FullName, DOB, Contact, Image, SpouseName, SpouseImage, etc. Add a status field (enum: Active, Undeployed, Deceased) reflecting their overall state. Remove “Pastoring Start” and “CameFromPastor” from this table, since these vary per assignment. Instead link history in a separate table.

Churches. Stores each congregation: churchId, name, location, districtId, status (Local/International), notes, etc. (User already has this.)

Districts. (districtId, name, themeColor, leaderPastorId, notes). Each district may have a lead pastor (foreign key to Pastors).

PastorAssignments. A new table to track each pastor’s term at a church (essentially the “history”):

assignmentId (PK)
pastorId (FK to Pastors)
churchId (FK to Churches)
districtId (FK, possibly same as church’s district)
startDate (MM/YYYY) and endDate (nullable)
assignmentType (enum: Pioneering, Takeover, Transferred, etc.) – describing how they began or the type of this pastorate.
notes field.
Optional: predecessorAssignmentId or previousPastorId to record who they replaced.
The endDate plus maybe an “endReason” (enum: Transferred, PulledOut, Redirection, etc.) record how the term ended.
By recording assignments this way, a pastor can have multiple assignments over time. For example, Greg Mitchell would have an assignment from Feb 2004 to present (Type=Pioneering, since he pioneered Prescott’s permanent building), while Jesse Morales would have at least two records (one in Santa Fe, one in Rochester, one in Prescott) with types Pioneering or Takeover as appropriate.

Pastors table “status”. We can set a pastor’s status to ACTIVE if they have a current (no-end-date) assignment, or DECEASED if marked as deceased, etc. “Undeployed” for pastors not currently assigned anywhere. (Alternatively, status can be derived from assignments, but a field simplifies queries.)

Disciples. (As given.) Link each disciple to a pastor, church, and district.

Assignments (alternative). The existing “ASSIGNMENTS” table is basically the same concept as above and can be augmented rather than a new table. It should include start date, optional end date, and now an assignmentType field and notes.

In summary, model people and pastorates separately: store each pastor once, and store each church-term separately. This lets us keep a full history of who served where and when, along with the reason (e.g. pioneering vs takeover) for each transition. (This mirrors real pastor bios which list each church they led
.)

Secretary UI and Workflows
The secretary interface should support managing all of the above entities and transitions:

Add/Edit Pastor: Enter personal details (name, contact, image, spouse info, DOB). Set initial status (e.g. “Undeployed” for new pastors). Ability to mark as draft vs final record if needed (per the old spec “Can: Record, Draft”).

View Pastor Profile: Show pastor info plus assignment history. For each assignment list church, dates, role (type), predecessor/successor. Example: show “Pastored Santa Fe Christian Fellowship (NM) [start–end] (Pioneering)”, “Pastored Potter’s House Rochester, NY [dates] (Takeover from Pastor X)”
.

Add Assignment (Pastor → Church): Choose a pastor and a church, enter a start date, end date (if historical), and select assignment type (Pioneering, Takeover, etc). Optionally specify the outgoing pastor (CameFrom) if takeover. Save this in the Assignments table. The pastor’s status can update to ACTIVE (if it’s their current term). The church’s record could also note the current pastor.

Historical Records: The secretary can enter old pastors by creating Pastor records with past assignments. In other words, to add a church’s history, first create the Pastors (if not already) then create Assignments for their terms (with past dates). For example, to record that Pastor X pioneered Church Y in 1990, add Pastor X, then an assignment (start=1990, type=Pioneering).

Delete/Archive: Rather than fully deleting pastors (which loses history), better to have an archive option or mark them inactive. But if deletion is needed, ensure cascading or manual removal of related assignments/disciple links.

Add/Remove Church: Secretary can add a new church (name, location, district, status). When creating or editing a church, they can assign the current pastor by creating an assignment. Removing a church might disable it or delete related data.

District Management: Secretary can create districts, assign a district lead pastor, pick theme colors, etc. They can assign/remove churches from a district as needed.

Disciple Management: Add a disciple and link to their pastor/church/district. This is straightforward given existing fields.

Reporting: Secretary views could include lists of current pastors (with statuses), lists of churches by district, etc. For example, a list of “Active Pastors” (status=Active) with their church, and a separate list of historical pastors.

Throughout, use the enumerated statuses. For example, on the pastor’s record allow selecting Active, Undeployed, Deceased. On assignments allow types Pioneering, Takeover, Transferred, Pulled-Out, Redirection, etc. The UI should validate that, for instance, a Pioneering assignment is only created for a new church (or mark it so).

Example: To record a new pastorate, the secretary would click “Add Assignment”, pick Pastor Jesse Morales and Church “The Potter’s House – Prescott AZ”, set start=MM/YYYY, type=Takeover (if he succeeded the previous pastor), and save. Jesse’s Pastors row shows status=Active, and Church Prescott lists Jesse as its current pastor. Jesse’s profile will then display past entries like “Pioneered Santa Fe CF (NM)” and “Pastored Rochester, NY” from [40†L133-L137].

Summary of Integration
By separating Pastors from PastorAssignments, we can easily keep current and historical data. Each time a pastor moves (pioneers a church, is appointed elsewhere, retires, etc.), we create or close an assignment with the appropriate status/reason. This mirrors how churches describe their leaders on websites (they list every church a pastor led and how, e.g. “pioneered… then assumed pastorate”
). Using this design, the secretary can add a pastor, attach him to his churches (current and former), and select the correct “Pioneering/Takeover/Transferred” type for each term. These structured records ensure a complete pastor history is maintained for reporting and church records.

Sources: Potter’s House church staff biographies
 illustrate how pastors “pioneered” new churches or “assumed” existing ones, which guided this design. The global PHCF network uses names like Victory Chapel and Christian Center interchangeably for its churches
, so all such congregations can be managed under one schema. (Biographies of founding pastors like Wayman Mitchell also confirm life events, e.g. his death in 2020
.)
